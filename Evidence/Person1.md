Room/property listings + discrimination/fairness
Preferences/restrictions based on gender, nationality, ethnicity, religion, age, etc.
The search page’s filters panel has a preference section with separate groups:
Race (Other, Indian, Chinese, Malay),
Nationality (Malaysian, non-Malaysian, etc), 
Status (Couple, Single female, Single male), 
Occupation (Unemployed, employed, student).
Tenants can filter search results by race. 
In Cheras, there are 1834 rooms listed. Filtering by ‘Other’ showed only 493 results. Indian showed only 590 rooms. Malay showed 897, and Chinese showed 1038.
In Bukit Jalil, there are 986 rooms listed. Choosing Indian only shows 175, ‘Other’ shows 243, Malay shows 308, Chinese shows 395.

Each listing has ‘tenant preferences’. Many listings have specific tags highlighting a race or nationality (i.e ‘Race = Malay’, ‘Race = Chinese’, ‘Malaysian Nationality’). Some listings specify descriptions such as ‘Prefer single chinese female’,Figure 3
The platform uses the word ‘preferences’ but these tags actively filter search results and, so, do not show listings equally to all users regardless of their background
Nationality: listings can be restricted to only accept Malaysians. There is a listing which requires no deposit from Malaysians, but requires one-month from non-Malaysians. (figures 7 and 8)
Occupation: listings can exclude unemployed people by only selecting the employed tag.
Gender: Gender tags are common in listings, but these may be defensible in the context of shared living, so they should be considered separately from race.
Misleading, incomplete, or potentially unfair listings
There are contradictory listings such as:
A listing says ‘Male unit’ in the title but the description says ‘female single room’Figure 4
Title says female unit but tags include ‘single male’ and ‘couple’Figures 5 and 6
Title says ‘Zero Deposit, 300RM booking only’ but description says “Malaysians: no deposit, Non-Malaysians: 1 month deposit” …. Figures 7 and 8 
Why this is relevant
There are clear signs that the platform’s design enables discrimination, and clear patterns of vague/inconsistent listings with incomplete/contradictory data.
Ethical Concerns
Equal access to housing, fairness, and the platform’s responsibility for the features it includes. Also, where does the line sit between potentially legitimate and discriminatory differential treatment (e.g. Gender-based VS Racial)?
